# One-Square-Kilometer Exploration Demo

From the repository root:

```sh
make release
./build/release/io --scene assets/villages/world.json
```

## Playing

- WASD: camera-relative walking, following terrain and stopping/sliding at obstacles.
- E: talk to the nearest living resident within 4 meters. Repeat for another line.
- Left-click: talk to a resident within reach, or target an enemy during combat.
- R: recruit a nearby companion. Repeating it does not duplicate party members.
- M: toggle full-map overview and the player-following local view.
- F: toggle camera follow; held arrow keys or right-drag orbit, middle-drag pans,
  scroll zooms. Orbit and WASD work simultaneously; WASD is remapped through the
  current camera's ground-plane basis every frame, independent of zoom.
- Visible raiders within 20 meters prepare combat automatically. Enter can also
  request detection, but cannot engage through buildings or hills.
- `ROUND BEGIN [ENTER]`: characters and the movement timer stay frozen until
  Enter confirms. This prompt appears before the first and each subsequent round.
- In combat: each confirmed round starts with 3 seconds of movement; 1/2 select an attack,
  click an enemy to execute, Space passes. Companions take their own NPC turns.

The player starts in Brookmere. Cora is nearby in blue/gold. Rowan waits in
Clover Hollow and Ivo in Highmeadow. Each companion has a home and can be
recruited independently. Follow the pale-green roads between settlements.

Only visible raiders within detection range and recruited companions within
30 meters enter combat, not the entire settlement through walls. Civilians
and the other settlements continue their schedules. Victory returns to exploration
after a short feedback delay. Health, recruitment and deaths persist in the same
world; a defeated raider does not respawn when the camera leaves. Player death
ends play; reopen the scene to reset. There is no disk save/load yet.

During movement, losing contact with every enemy for two continuous seconds
returns to wandering. Contact persists within 5 meters regardless of cover, or
within 30 meters with sight. This hysteresis prevents boundary flicker and brief
occlusion from cancelling a fight. Turns and projectiles finish normally; hiding
does not cancel a pending attack. Health and surviving enemies persist on escape.
Sight is omnidirectional actor-to-actor, not based on where the camera points.

Player and companion combat speed is now 5 m/s (previously 8), raiders 2.5 m/s.
Exploration uses the same player template speed; companions can catch up at 1.25x.
Edit `combat.templates.*.movement_speed` in the generator or your own scene to
tune pace. This is slower controlled movement, not added physical inertia.

## Content

The map is 1,000 by 1,000 meters, centered at the world origin. Terrain covers
[-500, 500] in X/Y using a 201 by 201 height field at 5-meter spacing. Rendering
is split into 400 terrain tiles so existing spatial/frustum culling still works.
Villages sit on flattened areas within the rolling terrain. Roads form a winding
loop through all three villages, with village exits avoiding house plots.

There are 751 placed Items, including 60 NPCs: 48 family residents, 3 companions,
and 9 raiders. Each settlement has four four-person households, a companion's
home, a shop, an inn and a civic hall. Shopkeepers, innkeepers and clerks are
members of those households and have outdoor work/home routes.

`assets/villages/dialogue.json` contains 200 distinct authored lines: 40 each
for men, women, children and parents, plus 20 shop and 20 civic staff lines.
NPCs can have multiple dialogue tags, for example parent + woman + shop staff.
Lines rotate deterministically on interaction; they are not generated by a
language model at runtime. Dialogue is flavor, not branching quests or trading.

## Ownership

`io-village` is another statically registered game plugin. It owns household
bindings, dialogue selection, routes, recruitment and encounter selection. It
composes `io-encounter` directly inside its existing `Session`, without starting
another worker or advancing the world twice. `src/game.rs` is application-level
plugin selection and presentation adaptation, not a dependency of engine crates.

The NPC's position and health remain on its world Item. The plugin stores only
route progress, dialogue cursor, household references, recruitment and follow
progress. Near-player residents update every fixed tick; residents farther than
80 meters advance their routes approximately once per simulated second. Neither
retention nor schedule advancement depends on camera visibility. This is a simple
route schedule, not a full day/night life simulation.

Companions follow a bounded trail of actual player positions rather than taking
a straight shortcut through every building. In combat they use the encounter's
NPC movement and attacks. New encounters use fresh round numbers so an old
movement-window command cannot move a character in a later encounter.

The optional `combat.confirm_round_start` selects the reusable `io-game` Ready
phase; legacy scenes default to immediate rounds. Ready has no movement window
and cannot advance its timer. `io-world::line_of_sight` handles geometry only:
segment tests against box/sphere colliders and terrain triangle boundaries.
The village plugin owns sight range, roster selection and disengagement rules.

## Grounded Movement and Limits

The optional `Grounded` Item component lives in `io-world`, not the village
plugin. It defines an upright footprint, height and maximum slope. The shared
checked `HeightField` uses exactly the terrain mesh's triangle interpolation.
World snapshots share immutable terrain through `Arc` rather than copying all
samples every tick. Plugins request movement through `ground_destination` and
checked placement; they do not mutate Item fields directly.

Movement is conservatively substepped against collider bounds and other grounded
characters, with axis sliding. It prevents thin-wall tunneling at supported
movement distances and stops at the map edge. It is not a general navmesh,
capsule sweep, jumping, stair climbing or a rigid-body pushing controller.
NPCs may queue behind each other; complex crowds and obstacles need better pathfinding.
Trees are decorative in this first map; building walls and characters block walking.

Living grounded characters have exclusive pose ownership, not a dynamic physics
body. On death, validated dynamic-body attachment removes Grounded and hands the
Item to existing rigid-body physics. Settlement combat areas have flat physical
floor colliders underneath the rendered ground. **The height field is not a new
rigid-body mesh collider**: corpses pushed out into distant hills do not have
arbitrary terrain collision support. Keep combat tests on settlement ground.

Models are simple static posed prototypes; movement is functional but limbs and
weapons do not animate. Buildings have no enterable interiors. Household and job
data do not yet implement economics, family relationships evolving over time,
quests, inventories, shops, persistence or network multiplayer.

## Editing and Checking

Edit dialogue or the content generator, then rebuild generated scene/assets:

```sh
python3 tools/build_villages.py
cargo run --offline --bin io-validate -- scene assets/villages/world.json
make test
```

The generator writes only `assets/villages` outputs, never the source drawing,
adventurer GLBs, other scenes, or the authored dialogue file. Changing content
does not require modifying framework code. Manual generated-scene edits should
use another filename if they need to survive regeneration.

`assets/villages/overview.json` opens the full map initially. Native captures
omit the HUD; interactive runs include dialogue and the existing FPS/tick display.
Tests exercise real generated assets, off-camera movement, snapshot independence,
recruit/follow/companion attacks, local encounter membership, movement locking,
return to exploration, preserved deaths, fresh round IDs, terrain sampling,
wall blocking, native input/HUD contracts, and the 200-line content contract.

## View Coverage and Dialogue

Village cameras use `camera.coverage` with `type: "viewport"` and world-space
`min_z`/`max_z` bounds, generated from the terrain plus a prop-height margin.
The render query radius expands with zoom, aspect ratio and pitch to cover that
height band. Frustum culling and existing screen-size LOD selection still apply.
The original `render_distance` remains the minimum render radius and the separate
simulation-region radius; zoom and map toggles do not increase that simulation
radius. Legacy scenes default to `{"type":"radius"}` and retain their explicit
distance cutoff. Custom taller/deeper content should extend the height band.

Authored mixed-case dialogue is rendered with the HUD's uppercase bitmap face;
common sentence punctuation is supported. Dialogue temporarily replaces debug
lines, wraps long words, and fits the existing 12-line/63-column presentation
contract. The underlying authored text is not modified.
